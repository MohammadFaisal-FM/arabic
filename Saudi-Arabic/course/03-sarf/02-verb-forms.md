# Lesson 3.2 — Verb Patterns (Forms I–X)

## What is a "form"?

The **root** gives core meaning. The **pattern** (form) changes how it's used — like English "write / rewrite / be written" but **regular and predictable**.

| Form | Name (brief) | Example root د-ر-س (study) |
|------|--------------|------------------------------|
| **I** | Basic | دَرَسَ (darasa) — he studied |
| **II** | Intensive / causative | دَرَّسَ — he taught |
| **III** | Mutual / with preposition feel | دَارَسَ — he studied with someone |
| **IV** | Causative | أَدْرَسَ — he made study |
| **V–X** | Advanced | learn as you meet them |

**Najdi focus:** Form **I** is 80%+ of daily speech. Learn II–IV when you see them.

---

## Form I pattern (past / present)

| Tense | Pattern (3-letter root) | Example ك-ت-ب |
|-------|-------------------------|---------------|
| Past (he) | فَعَلَ | كَتَبَ |
| Present (he) | يَفْعُلُ | يَكْتُبُ |

## English speaker tip

Don't memorize all 10 forms now. When you meet a new verb:

1. Identify the **root**
2. Ask: **Form I?** (most common)
3. `drill verb [verb]` for full table
