# Lesson 4.2 — Nominal vs Verbal Sentences

## Two sentence types

| Arabic term | English | Structure | Example |
|-------------|---------|-----------|---------|
| **جملة اسمية** | Nominal sentence | **noun-based** — no "is" needed | البيت **كبير** (The house [is] big) |
| **جملة فعلية** | Verbal sentence | **starts with verb** | **كتب** الولد (The boy wrote) |

---

## Nominal sentence (English speaker view)

| Arabic | Word-by-word | Natural English |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| أنا في البيت | I in house | I am in the house |
| الجد **تعب** | The grandfather tired | Grandpa is tired |
| أمي **طباخة** | My mother cook | My mom is a cook |

**Predicate** (second part) is often an **ism** (adjective, noun, prepositional phrase).

---

## Verbal sentence

| Arabic | English |
|--------|---------|
| أكل الولد | The boy ate |
| راحت أمي | My mother went |
| قلت لها | I said to her |

**Najdi:** subject order is flexible — context + verb form tell you who did what.
