# Lesson 0.1 — Welcome: Arabic vs English

## For English speakers

If you know English grammar (noun, verb, adjective, preposition), you already have a **map**. Arabic uses different labels and a **root system**, but the ideas overlap.

---

## Key differences

| English habit | Arabic reality |
|---------------|----------------|
| Words are mostly fixed (write → wrote → written) | Most words come from **3-letter roots** (ك-ت-ب → write, book, writer…) |
| Subject-Verb-Object order common | Flexible order; **verb often first** in formal Arabic |
| "a / the" (a, the) | Arabic has **definite article** ال only (no indefinite "a") |
| Tenses: past, present, future | Past + present; future often = present + **سـ** or **بـ** (Najdi) |
| Alphabet (Latin) | **Arabic script**, right-to-left |

---

## Two pillars of Arabic grammar

| Arabic term | English meaning | Question it answers |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **صرف** (Sarf) | Morphology / word formation | Where did this word **come from**? |
| **نحو** (Nahw) | Syntax / sentence grammar | How does this word **work in a sentence**? |

You will learn **Sarf first** (roots, verbs), then **Nahw** (sentences), then **Najdi** (how Saudis actually speak).

---

## Three word types (preview)

Every Arabic word is one of:

| Arabic | English | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **اسم** (ism) | Noun / name word | بيت (house), أنا (I) |
| **فعل** (fi'l) | Verb | كتب (he wrote), يكتب (he writes) |
| **حرف** (harf) | Particle | في (in), من (from), لا (no) |

Module 2 covers this in full.

---

## Your spoken Arabic

This course focuses on **everyday Arabic** as spoken in central Saudi Arabia (Riyadh, Qassim). It shares roots and grammar with **MSA** (Modern Standard Arabic) but uses different everyday words and pronunciation.

**Goal:** Talk with family — not news reading or classical poetry.
